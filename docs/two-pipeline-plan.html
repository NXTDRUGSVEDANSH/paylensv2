PayLens — Two-Pipeline Plan

Measurement and advisory, in one conversation.
Current architecture, the gaps, and the build strategy.

PayLens answers payment questions where every number traces to a validated, access-scoped, read-only query shown beside it. That works for “success rate by issuer last week”. It is the wrong shape for “how do I improve my success rate” — a question whose answer is a judgement, not a number. This document covers both paths, how a question is routed between them, and how they share one conversation.

The constraint that shapes every decision below. The model never computes. It picks an id from a closed set or writes prose that a deterministic checker can reject; Python does the arithmetic. A plausible invented benchmark — “most estates see 2–3% technical declines” — is the most damaging output this tool can produce, so no new LLM call is added anywhere a checker cannot reject its output.

1 · What exists today — three routes, not two

A question already takes one of three paths, decided by a chain of deterministic checks in backend/core/pipeline.py. Any design that assumes two paths will break the routing, because the health scan is a third.

2 · Pipeline A — measurement

The answer is the number. Substance comes from SQL, and a wrong answer is falsifiable by a query. Fifteen stages run after routing; the model appears in exactly three of them and never computes a value.

3 · Pipeline B — advisory (built, and isolated)

backend/core/advisory.py, 754 lines. The answer is a judgement: what to do, what a term means, whether a figure is good, what would happen. Substance comes from a declared catalogue plus facts measured from this estate. SQL is the evidence, not the answer.

The catalogue — semantic/playbooks.yaml, 487 lines

ContentsWhy it is YAML and not a prompt

The property worth protecting. A payments person can review and edit this file without reading a line of Python. Every proposal in section 6 preserves that.

The discriminator — one test, not a phrase list

Do not separate the pipelines by how a question is worded. Separate them by what the answer's substance is, and decide with a single question: can a wrong answer be falsified by a query?

RouteSubstance Falsifiable?Example
A · measureSQLyes success rate by issuer last week · GMV by method in October
B · advisecatalogue + measured factsno how do I improve success rate · should I retry · is 86% good
C · diagnose7 declared checksyes, per finding what problems does my system have
D · mixed newbothin part why did success rate drop and how do I fix it

Pipeline B's sub-kinds already implemented: definition, benchmark, recommendation, hypothetical. Two to add: procedure (a runbook — how do I actually do this) and sizing (what is this worth in rupees).

5 · Target architecture — the router

Three tiers. Deterministic rules decide what they can; a model is consulted only when the rules are undecided, and then it returns a label from a closed set and nothing else. Python decides what the label means — the same discipline that lets the planner pick a metric id.

The mixed route — one question with two halves

6 · One conversation across both pipelines

Currently at zero. Carry-over must be written as explicit rules, the way followup.py already has five, and every inherited field must be shown on the answer — an assumption the user cannot see is one they cannot correct.

The four rules, stated

  1. measure → advise. Inherit the window, the filters and the segment from the previous answered turn. Today the advisory path always calls _scan_window and so answers about the wrong period.
  2. advise → measure. Each lever's measure question becomes resolvable context, so an ordinal reference works.
  3. advise → advise. Window and catalogue ids carry; the kind does not.
  4. Inheritance only ever fills a gap. A question that stands on its own is answered exactly as typed.

7 · More LLM calls — and the rule that earns them

Add a call only where a deterministic checker can reject its output. This is what separates an LLM call from a liability. Six slots qualify; three exist, three are new. One of the new ones is the auto-healing call.
Latency is the real cap, not correctness. On the current free-tier model each call costs roughly 11 seconds. Four extra calls on the advisory path is ~40s worst case. That number — not safety — decides how many of these slots get filled.

8 · External knowledge — curated, not crawled

“Auto-fetch business manuals and feed them in” is right about the problem and dangerous as stated: a scraped payments corpus is full of industry benchmarks, which is precisely the output this tool must never produce. The invariant that makes ingestion safe:

External knowledge may supply levers, definitions, procedures and causal mechanisms. It may never supply a number about this estate, and never an industry benchmark presented as fact.
One stated principle needs your sign-off. Retrieval over a corpus is similarity ranking, which the project bans. I would keep it banned for choosing a playbook, and allow it for finding supporting text that must then be cited as [K*]. That is an amendment to a documented invariant, not an implementation detail.

9 · Dataset expansion, phases, and what is needed

Pipeline B is weakest where the data has no column for the lever it is recommending. Three additions, in order of what they unlock:

AdditionFields What becomes answerable
1 · Retry outcomes original_txn_id, attempt_no Retry advice is currently inferred from an is_retriable flag — whether a retry ever worked cannot be measured. Turns the retry playbook from advice into a measured outcome and closes the standing caveat that attempts are not linked to intent.
2 · Auth / 3DS step auth_method, otp_attempts, pre-auth drop-off The journey has an authentication step with nothing behind it. Checkout and auth friction is the largest real-world success-rate lever and is presently unaddressable.
3 · Routing decisions candidate_acquirers, chosen_acquirer, route_reason The headline demo blames routing and the data cannot see routing. Makes the hypothetical kind a counterfactual instead of a projection.
Lower priorityMDR per attempt · repeat-vs-first-time identity · issuer downtime windows Lets advice be ranked by money rather than by volume.

Keep the NPCI calibration discipline and the [MODELLED] / [ASSUMED] labelling in datasets/CALIBRATION.md for every new share.
